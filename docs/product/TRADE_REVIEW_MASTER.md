# Master Trade Review — Small-Cap Momentum

A question-based daily and trade review, adapted from the supplied framework attributed to Lance Breitstein, the Good Trade Checklist, and our small-cap momentum review discussion.

> **“No named setup” and “no defined trade” are different things.**

## How to use this questionnaire

- Complete **Part A once for the day**, then repeat **Part B for selected trades or ticker sequences**. Carry the day's context into each trade; only note differences.
- Start with the **core questions**. Short answers are enough. Use the optional follow-ups when they help explain a decision; this is a question bank, not a requirement to answer every prompt.
- Add a question anywhere when something deserves more attention. Keep useful recurring additions in this master file.
- Keep tags alongside the answers for classification and retrieval. A named setup or a perfect tag is not required to explain a trade.
- Separate what you knew at the decision point from what you learned afterward. “Not defined,” “not observed,” and “not sure” are useful answers.
- Evaluate opportunity quality, execution quality, and financial outcome separately. Include disciplined losses, good passes, and well-executed winners.

## Part A — The Day

### 1. How did the day go?

**Core questions**

1. How did the day go, beyond whether you made or lost money?
2. What was your single process focus today, and how well did you follow it?

**Response:**

**Optional follow-ups**

- What was your best decision today, including a decision to wait or pass?
- Did frustration, FOMO, hesitation, fatigue, or an earlier win or loss change how you traded?
- Did you follow your existing daily risk and stopping rules?

### 2. What was the market context?

**Core questions**

1. What was the small-cap market like during your trading window?
2. How many meaningful movers were there, and which names stood out?
3. Did your trade frequency and position size fit the opportunity available?

**Response:**

**Optional follow-ups**

- Were breakouts following through, repeatedly failing, or mixed?
- Were multiple stocks making sustained moves, or was opportunity concentrated in one or two names?
- Were leaders making meaningful moves, or were you repeatedly fighting for a few cents?
- Did opportunity improve or fade as the session progressed, and did you adapt?
- Did SPY, QQQ, sector activity, or a broader news event actually influence your decisions?
- Which trades, repeated attempts, missed opportunities, or passes deserve a closer look?

Use a rough mover count if needed. Name the watchlist or scanner universe and trading window when known; do not imply a complete market count.

## Part B — The Trade or Ticker Sequence

**Trade details:** Ticker: ___ · Direction: ___ · Entry/exit times (ET): ___ · Net realized P&L: ___ · Chart/replay links: ___

### 3. How would you classify the opportunity and setup?

**Core question**

1. What kind of opportunity were you trading: an initial burst, a second opportunity, or another situation?

**Response:**

**Optional follow-ups**

- Was your entry based on a recognizable setup, an immediate price-action or tape signal, or both?
- If there was a named setup, what was it?
- Were you intending a quick scalp or a longer continuation trade?
- Were you participating early in the move, in a later continuation, or after an earlier failed attempt?

**Optional tags:** Opportunity phase: ___ · Setup, if applicable: ___ · Entry basis: ___ · Trading period: ___

An **initial burst** describes the first sudden move or acceleration being considered. A **second opportunity** describes a trade after that move has revealed more information, such as a pullback, consolidation, curl, reclaim, or renewed break. Either may involve a tape or price-action entry. The phase describes the stock's move; it does not indicate how many times you personally traded it. A second opportunity may be your first attempt.

### 4. What was the stock and chart context?

**Core question**

1. What made this stock worth your attention, and which levels or conditions mattered at entry?

**Response:**

**Optional follow-ups**

- Was there a verified catalyst, fresh news, a sympathy move, continued interest from an earlier move, or no known catalyst?
- When did you learn about the catalyst, and what was the source?
- What did the daily chart or prior sessions reveal about relevant resistance, supply, or support?
- Where were the intraday levels you actually used, such as premarket high, high of day, VWAP, or a whole/half-dollar level?
- Did float, relative volume, accumulated volume, recent supply-related news, or halt history affect your decision?
- Which annotated chart or replay moment best shows the context you saw?

Attach daily and intraday charts when useful. Mark entries, adds, trims, and exits. Record sources/as-of times for stock-profile facts when available, and distinguish later discoveries from entry-time knowledge.

### 5. What was the trade thesis?

**Core questions**

1. What specifically prompted the entry?
2. What movement did you expect next?
3. What would invalidate that expectation?
4. Was there enough room left to justify the risk?
5. How were you planning to get out?

**Response:**

**Optional follow-ups**

- Where was your first logical profit target, and what resistance or supply stood in the way?
- Was your invalidation based on the setup and price action, or an arbitrary number of cents?
- How quickly did you expect follow-through, and what would a lack of progress mean under your plan?
- For an initial burst, what changed in price, participation, or tape that made you act?
- For a second opportunity, what did the first move and subsequent reaction reveal that supported this entry?

Describe the thesis you actually had. If an exit or invalidation was undefined, record that rather than constructing a plan afterward.

### 6. How good was the opportunity at entry?

**Core question**

1. Did this trade meet your Good Trade Checklist: room, defined risk, participation, clean price action, momentum, and suitable market conditions?

**Response:**

**Optional follow-ups — opportunity and room**

- Was there realistically $0.10+ of tradable movement available from your entry?
- Was there $0.20–$0.30+ potential if the trade worked, and what supported that expectation?
- Did the potential reward justify the risk, or were you risking $0.10 to capture $0.05?
- Were you entering while the opportunity was developing, or chasing an already extended move?
- Were you trading a meaningful move, or trying to manufacture a trade from micro-moves?

**Optional follow-ups — volume, price action, and tape**

- Was meaningful volume coming in, and was participation expanding or fading?
- Was RVOL supportive, if you had that information?
- Was there enough liquidity for your intended size, and was the spread reasonable relative to the intended move?
- Were levels being respected, pullbacks controlled, or higher lows forming where relevant?
- Was the price action clean enough to define risk, or choppy and whippy?
- Did the tape feel alive, and what observable behavior supported that impression?
- Were offers getting taken and buyers becoming more aggressive, or was activity stalling?

The cent benchmarks above come from your checklist; they are review criteria, not a claim that every qualifying move is tradable. Judge using evidence available at entry. Follow-through observed only after entry belongs in execution review. Distinguish recorded tape evidence from memory or interpretation; a candle does not establish Level 2 behavior.

### 7. How well did you execute the trade?

**Core questions**

1. What actually happened after entry, and how did you respond compared with your original thesis?
2. Was your position size appropriate for the opportunity, liquidity, existing risk limits, and P&L cushion at the time?

**Response:**

**Optional follow-ups — entry and management**

- Did you enter where you intended, hesitate, anticipate without sufficient evidence, or chase?
- Did the expected buying, price response, or breakout follow-through appear?
- Did you respect invalidation when the expected behavior failed?
- Did your adds, trims, and final exit match the trade you originally intended?
- If you changed the plan, what new evidence justified it at the time?
- Did fear, FOMO, frustration, or the desire for a larger winner affect your decisions?

**Optional follow-ups — size and cushion**

- Without a cushion, did you use your default or reduced size as intended by your checklist?
- With a cushion, did the opportunity justify increased size, or did being green become the reason to increase it?
- How much of the cushion present at entry was at risk, and could one loss have erased most of it?

**Optional follow-ups — mechanics**

- Did spreads, slippage, partial fills, rejected orders, latency, or hotkey mistakes affect the trade?
- If an execution error occurred, what caused it and what concrete adjustment could address it?
- What quote, order record, screenshot, or replay supports your assessment?

**Optional assessment:** Opportunity quality: ___ · Execution quality: ___ · Supporting evidence: ___

Use a short description or your preferred grade. A profitable trade can have poor process, and a losing trade can have good process. Judge a scalp exit against the original intent and evidence available then, rather than the stock's eventual high. If reporting R, retain the original documented risk convention; actual fill prices already affect P&L, so do not deduct slippage twice.

**Optional follow-ups**

- Did you stop when the opportunity no longer met your criteria?

Count each flat-to-flat position as an attempt; record adds and trims within that attempt. Show sequence P&L in dollars so earlier losses remain visible. Do not combine R values with different risk denominators into an unexplained sequence R.

### 9. What would you repeat or change?

**Core question**

1. What is the most useful lesson from this trade or sequence, and what specific action follows from it?

**Response:**

**Optional follow-ups — best executable alternative**

- Using only information available then, what would better execution have looked like?
- What observable cue would have supported waiting, entering, reducing, exiting, or passing?
- Was that alternative realistic at your size and available prices?

**Optional follow-ups — peers, sourcing, and further study**

- Did a peer or mentor see something useful, and were their holding horizon and risk approach comparable to yours?
- Could a scanner, alert, or watchlist change have surfaced this opportunity more effectively?
- Does the lesson suggest a hypothesis to study across more trades before changing a rule?
- What evidence would support or contradict that hypothesis?

## Framework notes

This file consolidates the earlier draft and subsequent discussion as the master questionnaire. It preserves the supplied Lance framework's progression through classification, market and stock context, charts and tape, execution and psychology, and actionable learning. Peer comparison and opportunity sourcing remain available as optional deeper questions.

The small-cap adaptation adds explicit opportunity phases, the five-question trade thesis, the Good Trade Checklist, cushion-aware sizing review, and repeated-attempt review. “Theoretical ideal execution” becomes a question about the best executable decision using information available at the time. Named setups remain optional; a clear trade premise remains central.
