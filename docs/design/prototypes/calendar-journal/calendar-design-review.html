Calendar — design review

What the month view should surface, how a day opens, and which parts already exist. August 7, 2026.

The calendar's job is not to show data — the journal does that. Its job is to let a trader scan a month in five seconds, spot the day that needs review, and get to it in one click. Every decision below follows from that.

Starting point

  • The journal prototype (Journal Day v2) already has a month grid: Mon–Fri columns, P&L + trade count per cell, click navigates to the day view.
  • The design system specs CalendarCell: four states, a trailing week-total column, no status dots and no filled cell backgrounds.
  • The day level is largely built: trade table grid, intraday P&L chart, ticker rail, stat row. Whatever opens from a cell can reuse these as-is.

In the cell — legible at arm's length

  • Day number
  • Signed P&L — the one number that matters
  • Trade count
  • State: no-trade day vs. not-yet-imported

Behind the click — the day peek

  • Accuracy and profit factor
  • Per-ticker P&L (ticker rail)
  • Trade-note tags, rule breaks
  • Note / coach-review status
  • The trades themselves

One open tension: the journal prototype marks the selected day with an accent dot; the CalendarCell spec bans status dots. If "note written" or "rule break" deserves cell-level presence, it needs a sanctioned form — a labelled glyph or a text mark, not a bare colored dot.

Opening a day

Four models for the click on August 4.

A · Inline expander

Click August 4 → a band slides open below that week: day stat row, trade table, "Open in journal." Click again or Esc collapses.

Stays in month context; reuses the trade table module directly. Cost: pushes lower weeks down; one day open at a time.

B · Docked day panel

The grid keeps its shape; a panel docks right with stats, ticker rail, and a compact trade list. Arrow keys walk days.

Grid never reflows; great for day-to-day comparison. Cost: panel is too narrow for the full 10-column table.

→

C · Zoom through

The cell is pure navigation: click goes straight to the day view already built in the journal. The calendar itself stays passive.

Zero new UI; one canonical day surface. Cost: loses month context on every look; back-and-forth to compare days.

D · Peek, then go

Hover or first click shows a light popover — P&L, accuracy, top tickers, note status — with "Open day" as the real action.

Cheapest scan of many days. Cost: hover UI is fragile, invisible on touch, and popovers are rare in this product.

Working recommendation: A, with C as its escape hatch. The inline expander matches your instinct — click August 4, the week slides open, and the existing trade table module drops in with a day stat row above it. "Open in journal" at the band's foot hands off to the full day view for charts, notes, and coach. B is the strongest alternative if comparing adjacent days matters more than reading full trade detail in place.

Other visualizations worth weighing

Week-total column

The sixth column from the CalendarCell spec: week P&L + accuracy. Cheap, already designed, answers "which week went wrong" without a chart.

Month equity line

PnlChart fed daily closes, sitting above the grid beside the month stat row. Gives the month a shape the grid alone can't.

Weekday DivBars

Signed P&L per weekday (Mon–Fri) from the Reports vocabulary. Surfaces "Mondays bleed" — a real coaching input.

Rule-break counts

Neutral HBars of rule-break tags per week. Counts, not outcomes — stays out of red/green by design.

Year mini-months

Twelve tiny grids with the one sanctioned green/red tint per day. The zoom-out level above month; likely a later phase.

Constraints that rule ideas out: red/green mean outcome only, so no heat-map saturation scales; no color-only encoding, so every mark pairs with a sign or label; flat surfaces, so no floating overlay charts.

Decided

Answered August 7. Built in Calendar Month.

Opening a day

Model A, the inline expander. Click a traded day, a band slides open beneath that week; click again to collapse.

Inside the band

Day stat row, then the light ledger — time, symbol, context tag, P&L. "Show all" expands; "Open in journal" hands off.

Cell contents

Day number, signed P&L, trade count and accuracy. No note or rule-break marks — the no-dots rule holds.

Month header

Stat row only: sessions, trades, accuracy, profit factor on the left; P&L pushed right, over the week-total column. The equity curve moved to Analytics.

Zoom

Month and Year only. Year draws twelve tinted mini-months with readable day numbers; click one to open it. Previous / Next / Today walk both levels.

Date ranges belong to Analytics, not the calendar

What the market does, and why we are not copying all of it.

Question raised: should the calendar have a From–To range filter, and what would selecting several months even render? Looking at how the established journals handle it:

  • None of them render an arbitrary range as calendars. The calendar only ever draws real calendar units — one month in detail, or a fixed twelve-month year.
  • Tradervue's year view is twelve full mini-months with an "Open" per month and a year switcher — the same structure as our Year tab.
  • Their range filter lives on a separate Reports page, alongside symbol, tags, side and duration. When a range is applied, the calendar keeps its full shape and days outside the range simply lose their color.
  • Calendar and Reports are separate items in their sidebar. The calendar is navigation; the filtered calendar is one report among many.

Our position: the calendar stays a navigation surface — month, year, previous/next, no filters. Previous and next already reach any year, so no separate year picker is needed. Arbitrary ranges and cross-cutting filters are an Analytics problem, and Analytics is its own project.

A note on why we are drawing that line: the reference product puts a filter bar, three report selectors, a tab row and a view switcher above the same grid, and jumps between report and calendar modes without warning. It is a cockpit. Depth is worth building — later, deliberately, on a page that is about depth.

Still open

  1. Week-total column — kept in the build as a toggle, not yet committed.
  2. Keyboard navigation between days once a band is open.
  3. Whether a no-trade day should be confirmable as a rest day from the cell, or only from the journal.
  4. Analytics: filter vocabulary, report types, and where the equity curve lands. A separate project.
